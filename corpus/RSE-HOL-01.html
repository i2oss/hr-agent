1 How Holidays Work

1.1 Your calendar follows your home office

Your company holidays follow the calendar of your home office, the office on your HR record, not the place where you are working or travelling. Employees of the Denver, Austin and Seattle offices follow the US calendar (§2). Employees of the Toronto office follow the Canada calendar (§3). An Austin employee working in Toronto for a week still follows the US calendar, and a Toronto employee visiting Denver still follows the Canada calendar.

1.2 Weekend holidays

When a holiday falls on a Saturday, it is observed on the Friday before. When it falls on a Sunday, it is observed on the Monday after. In Canada, when Christmas Day or Boxing Day falls on a weekend, each moves to the next working days. The tables below list the observed dates, which are the days the office is closed.

Only the observed date is a day off. If you are scheduled to work on the actual date because it falls on a weekend, for example on a project site, the substitute-day rule in §1.4 applies. The HR Portal shows each year's observed dates on your time-off calendar.

1.3 Holidays during PTO

A company holiday that falls inside your PTO dates is not deducted from your PTO. When you count the PTO days a request will use, leave out weekends and your home office's company holidays. For example, a Denver employee taking Wednesday to Friday off in a week where Wednesday is a US company holiday uses 2 PTO days, not 3. See also RSE-PTO-01 §3.2.

1.4 Part-time and field staff

Part-time employees are paid for a company holiday if it falls on one of their scheduled working days. Field/site-based employees who are asked to work on a company holiday get a substitute day off within 30 calendar days, agreed with their manager.

A part-time employee whose scheduled days don't include the holiday is not given another day off in its place. Substitute days for field staff are recorded in the HR Portal and are not deducted from PTO; if the substitute day isn't taken within the window, talk to your manager and HR.

4 Floating Holidays

Full-time and part-time employees in the US offices get 2 floating holidays a year; employees in the Toronto office get 1 floating holiday a year. Interns do not get floating holidays. Request a floating holiday like PTO, in the HR Portal; it is not deducted from your PTO balance. Your manager approves it. Floating holidays do not carry over: unused floating holidays lapse at the end of the year.

Floating holidays let you mark days that matter to you, such as a religious or cultural holiday that isn't a company holiday, a birthday, or a day such as Martin Luther King Jr. Day for US staff. They are taken as whole days. New employees receive their floating holidays for the year they join, and they are not paid out when you leave.

5 Observances

5.1 Remembrance Day (Toronto)

On November 11 the Toronto office observes two minutes of silence at 11:00. Remembrance Day is a regular working day for the Toronto office, not a company holiday, so a Toronto employee taking PTO on November 11 is charged a PTO day for it. (For the US offices, November 11 is Veterans Day, a company holiday; see §2.)

Toronto colleagues working with US teams that week should expect the US offices to be closed on November 11, and US staff should not assume their Toronto colleagues are off. Staff of any office who wish to join the Toronto observance remotely are welcome to do so.
