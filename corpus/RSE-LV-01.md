## 1 Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

This policy covers time away from work for reasons other than vacation: sick time, parental leave, bereavement, jury and witness duty, unpaid personal leave, and medical leave and accommodations. It applies to full-time and part-time employees. Interns are covered by sick time (§2), bereavement leave (§4) and jury and witness duty (§5) only.

Ridgeline wants people to be able to take the time they need for illness, family and civic duties without worrying about their job. Managers support colleagues on leave by planning cover early, keeping the reason for an absence private, and making the return to work straightforward. Leave rules are applied the same way in every office, and Canadian employees should also read §3.6.

### 1.2 Leave vs PTO

The leave types in this policy are separate from paid time off. They do not use your PTO balance unless this policy says so; the one exception is unpaid personal leave, which requires you to use your available PTO first (§6). PTO itself is covered by the Paid Time Off Policy (RSE-PTO-01).

Leave is requested through the HR Portal, where you choose the leave type. HR keeps the details of your leave, and your manager sees only the dates you will be away. If you aren't sure which kind of leave fits your situation, contact the HR Service Desk and HR will help you choose.

## 2 Sick Time

Ridgeline gives full-time employees 6 days of paid sick time a year. Sick time is front-loaded each January 1; in the year you are hired it is pro-rated by month. Part-time employees receive sick time pro-rated by their scheduled hours, the same way as PTO. Interns receive 3 days of sick time. Use sick time when you are ill or need to care for an ill family member, and tell your manager as early as you can. You don't need to tell your manager any medical details. If you are off sick for more than 3 consecutive days, send a note to HR (not to your manager). Unused sick time does not carry over into the next year. Sick time needs no approval; just notify your manager.

Record sick days in the HR Portal when you return. If you run out of sick time, further sick days can be taken as PTO, or HR can discuss unpaid personal leave or medical leave with you (§6, §7.1). Sick time is not paid out when you leave Ridgeline. Please don't come to the office or a project site when you are unwell; working remotely while ill is not expected.

## 3 Parental Leave

### 3.1 Eligibility

Full-time and part-time employees with at least 6 months of service are eligible for parental leave. It is available to any parent, for the birth of a child, an adoption or a foster placement. Interns and contractors are not eligible.

Service is counted from your hire date to the first day of your leave. Both parents may take parental leave if both work at Ridgeline. If you will reach 6 months of service shortly after the birth or placement, contact HR to discuss timing.

### 3.2 Pay and length

Eligible employees get 12 weeks of paid parental leave at 100% of base pay. HR approves parental leave, and your manager is notified.

The 12 weeks are calendar weeks, so company holidays inside your leave don't extend it. Parental leave is paid through normal payroll on your usual pay dates. You can add PTO before or after your parental leave in the usual way.

### 3.3 When to take it

Parental leave must be taken within 12 months of the birth, adoption or placement. You can take it in one continuous block, or in two blocks of at least 4 weeks each. Agree the dates with HR and let your manager know the plan.

For example, you could take 8 weeks after a birth and the remaining 4 weeks later in the year. Leave not taken within the 12 months lapses and is not paid out.

### 3.4 Notice

When the leave is foreseeable, give HR at least 30 calendar days' notice of your planned start date. If the timing changes unexpectedly, tell HR as soon as you can.

Request parental leave in the HR Portal. HR confirms your dates, your pay during leave and your expected return date, and lets your manager know so cover can be planned.

### 3.5 Benefits and PTO during parental leave

Your benefits continue on the same terms during parental leave, and your employee contributions continue through payroll (see also RSE-BEN-01 §4). Your PTO keeps accruing while you are on paid parental leave (RSE-PTO-01 §2.5). If your leave spans December 31, you can carry over more unused PTO than usual; see RSE-PTO-01 §5.2.

If you want to add a new child to your benefits, do it within the qualifying life event window in RSE-BEN-01 §3.4. Your HR record, Employment Type and Work Arrangement stay the same during parental leave, and you return to the same role or a similar one.

### 3.6 Canadian employees

For employees of the Toronto office, HR coordinates Ridgeline's parental leave with the employee's government parental benefits. Plan details are available from HR.

Contact HR as early as you can, because government benefits have their own application steps and timing. HR explains how Ridgeline's paid leave and government benefits fit together for your dates; it doesn't give personal advice on government programmes.

## 4 Bereavement Leave

If a member of your family dies, you can take paid bereavement leave: up to 5 days for an immediate family member (spouse or partner, child, parent, sibling, or a grandparent you lived with) and up to 2 days for an extended family member (grandparent, grandchild, aunt, uncle, in-law). HR approves bereavement leave, and your manager is notified. Talk to HR if you need more time; you can add PTO or request unpaid personal leave.

Bereavement days don't need to be taken consecutively; for example, you may take some days immediately and another for a funeral or memorial later. You don't need to provide documents unless HR asks.

## 5 Jury and Witness Duty

If you are called for jury duty or as a witness in court, Ridgeline pays you for up to 10 days. Give HR a copy of the summons, and tell your manager as soon as you receive it. HR approves the leave, and your manager is notified.

If you are released early on a day of jury service, you are not expected to return to work that day. Service beyond the paid days can be covered by PTO or unpaid personal leave; talk to HR.

## 6 Unpaid Personal Leave

You can request unpaid personal leave of up to 30 calendar days for personal reasons that PTO doesn't cover. HR approves unpaid personal leave. You must use your available PTO before unpaid leave begins. PTO accrual is paused during unpaid leave. If your unpaid leave will last more than 30 days, contact HR before the leave starts to arrange your benefits.

Request unpaid personal leave in the HR Portal with your dates and a short reason; you don't need to share personal details beyond what HR needs to decide. HR considers the effect on your project and team and talks to your manager about cover. Unpaid personal leave is not a substitute for medical leave or accommodations, which follow §7.

## 7 Medical Leave and Accommodations

### 7.1 Medical leave

Medical leave beyond sick time is arranged with HR directly. You share medical information only with HR. Your manager is told only the dates you will be away and any adjustments to your work.

Contact HR as soon as you know you will need medical leave, or ask someone to contact HR for you. HR will explain the options, the documents needed and how your pay and benefits are handled while you are away. Requests are handled through HR Confidential where you prefer.

### 7.2 Accommodations

If you need a workplace accommodation for a disability, a health condition or pregnancy, contact HR directly through HR Confidential or the HR Service Desk. HR will talk with you about what you need, keep your information confidential, and tell your manager only about the work adjustments, not the reason for them.

Accommodations might include changes to your schedule, equipment, workspace or duties. You can ask at any time, including before you join. HR may ask for information from a medical professional where it is needed to understand what will help, and will explain why.

## 8 Returning from Leave

When you return from parental, medical or unpaid personal leave, you will have a return-to-work check-in with your manager and HR to plan your first weeks back and any changes to your schedule.

The check-in covers your workload, any project changes while you were away, and any adjustments you need. HR arranges it before your return date. If you want to change your schedule or Work Arrangement when you return, raise it at the check-in; changes follow the usual approvals in the Approval Matrix (RSE-APR-01).
