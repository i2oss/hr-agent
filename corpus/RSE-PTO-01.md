## 1 Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Paid time off (PTO) is time away from work, at full pay, for vacation and personal reasons. This policy explains how PTO is earned, used, requested and carried over. Sick time and other kinds of leave, such as parental, bereavement and jury duty leave, are not PTO; they are covered by the Leave of Absence Policy (RSE-LV-01).

Ridgeline wants everyone to take their PTO. Time away from project deadlines keeps people healthy and makes for better engineering, so managers are expected to encourage their teams to plan time off through the year rather than saving it all for December. Company holidays and floating holidays are separate from PTO and are covered by the Holiday Calendar & Observance policy (RSE-HOL-01).

### 1.2 Who is eligible

Full-time employees (exempt and non-exempt), part-time employees and interns earn PTO under this policy. Contractors do not earn PTO and this policy does not apply to them: their time off is set by their contract or agency, and questions go to their agency or Ridgeline contract manager (RSE-HB-01 §1.2).

Eligibility is based on the Employment Type on your HR record. If your Employment Type changes during the year, for example from intern to full-time, your PTO from that date follows the rules for your new type.

## 2 Accrual

### 2.1 Full-time accrual

Full-time employees earn PTO according to their completed years of service, counted on January 1 (or from the hire date in the first year). With 0–2 completed years you earn 15 days a year. With 3–5 completed years you earn 20 days a year. With 6 or more completed years you earn 25 days a year. The higher rate starts in the calendar year after you reach the new band.

For example, an engineer who joined Ridgeline four years ago has 4 completed years of service and earns 20 days a year. An engineer who completes their sixth year of service during the year moves to 25 days a year from the following January 1. Service is counted from your hire date on your HR record, and periods of unpaid leave do not reduce it.

### 2.2 Part-time accrual

Part-time employees earn PTO pro rata. Take the full-time days for your service band, multiply by your scheduled weekly hours divided by 40, and round to the nearest half day. For example, a part-time employee scheduled for 24 hours a week in the 15-day band earns 9 days a year.

If your scheduled hours change during the year, your accrual changes from the next pay period. PTO already accrued is not recalculated. Part-time employees take PTO in the same half-day increments as everyone else, counted against their scheduled working days.

### 2.3 Interns

Interns earn 5 days of PTO per internship, whatever its length. Unused intern PTO is not carried over or paid out.

Interns request PTO in the HR Portal like everyone else, and the notice and approval rules in §4 apply. An intern who later joins Ridgeline as a full-time or part-time employee starts accruing under §2.1 or §2.2 from their new hire date.

### 2.4 When PTO accrues

PTO accrues each semi-monthly pay period, 24 periods a year. Each period you earn your annual days divided by 24. You can use PTO as soon as it has accrued.

This means your balance grows steadily through the year rather than arriving all at once on January 1. A new employee can take PTO early in their employment, but only the days already accrued; plan longer breaks for later in the year, when more PTO is available. Your available balance in the HR Portal already allows for days that are pending approval.

### 2.5 Accrual during leave

PTO keeps accruing while you are on paid leave, such as paid parental leave. PTO does not accrue during unpaid leave, such as unpaid personal leave (RSE-LV-01 §6).

PTO accrued before and during paid leave stays in your balance, and you can use it when you return or add it to the end of your leave with the usual approval. If your leave crosses the end of the year, see §5.2 for the higher carryover cap.

## 3 Using PTO

### 3.1 Increments

PTO is taken in whole or half days. The minimum increment is a half day (0.5 days).

A half day is a morning or an afternoon of your normal working day. If you need a shorter absence, such as an hour for an appointment, agree it informally with your manager instead of booking PTO. When your available balance includes a half day, you can use it on its own, for example to take one full day and one half day.

### 3.2 Company holidays inside PTO

A company holiday that falls inside your PTO dates is not deducted from your PTO balance. Only your scheduled working days are charged. Which days are company holidays depends on your home office calendar; see the Holiday Calendar & Observance policy (RSE-HOL-01 §1.3).

When you enter dates in the HR Portal, it works out the PTO days for you using your home office's calendar. For example, for an employee of a US office, a request for Wednesday to Friday in a week where Wednesday is a US company holiday is charged as 2 PTO days. The same request from an employee of the Toronto office may be charged differently, because the Toronto office follows the Canada calendar.

### 3.3 Your balance

Your PTO balance cannot go negative. You can only take PTO you have already accrued. If your request is longer than your available balance, you can reduce the request, or request unpaid personal leave for the remainder (RSE-LV-01 §6).

The HR Portal will not let you submit a request that takes your balance below zero. Managers cannot approve PTO that hasn't accrued yet, and Ridgeline does not advance PTO from future pay periods. If you are short of PTO for an important reason, talk to your manager and HR early; unpaid personal leave is approved by HR.

### 3.4 Checking your balance

You can see your balance in HR Portal > Time Off. The balance shown is as of the last pay period, and it lists days accrued, days used, days pending approval and any days carried over from last year.

Your available balance is days accrued plus days carried over, minus days used and days pending approval. Check it before you plan a longer break. If you think your balance is wrong, contact the HR Service Desk with the dates in question.

## 4 Requesting and Approval

### 4.1 How to request

Submit every PTO request in the HR Portal before the first day off. Tell your team and update your calendar once it is approved.

It helps to talk to your manager before submitting a longer request, especially around project deadlines or site milestones. Your request shows your dates, the PTO days charged and your remaining balance. Your manager is notified and approves or declines it in the HR Portal; you are notified of the decision. PTO is not approved until the HR Portal shows it as approved.

### 4.2 Notice

Requests for more than 3 PTO days need at least 14 calendar days' notice (two weeks) before the first day off. Requests of 3 PTO days or fewer have no minimum notice: submit them as early as practical. Count only the PTO days charged, not weekends or company holidays.

Notice is counted from the day you submit the request to the first day off. For example, a request submitted on a Monday for a full working week off starting two Mondays later meets the notice rule; the same request submitted the Friday before does not. A late request is not automatically declined, but your manager may decline it if the team can't cover your work.

### 4.3 Who approves

Your manager approves your PTO requests. A request for more than 10 consecutive PTO days must also be approved by your director. See the Approval Matrix (RSE-APR-01 §2).

Consecutive PTO days are counted as PTO days charged in one continuous absence; weekends and company holidays inside the absence don't count toward the total but don't break it either. Approvers consider project needs and team cover, and they should respond promptly so you can make plans.

### 4.4 Field-role December blackout

Field/site-based employees may not take PTO during the last two weeks of December, from December 18 to December 31, because of year-end site work. A field/site-based employee who needs PTO in that window must get a director's exception; the request goes to the director for approval.

Company holidays in the window, such as Christmas Day, are still company holidays. The blackout does not apply to office-hybrid or remote-approved employees, whose December PTO follows the normal rules. Field/site-based employees planning December time off should talk to their manager and director early in the autumn.

### 4.5 Changing or cancelling PTO

You can cancel or change a request in the HR Portal. Approved PTO that you cancel before it starts is restored to your balance.

To extend approved PTO, submit a new request for the extra days; the notice and approval rules apply to the whole absence. If you need to come back early, tell your manager and change the request so the unused days are restored.

## 5 Carryover and Payout

### 5.1 Carryover cap

You can carry over up to 5 unused PTO days into the next calendar year. On December 31, any unused days above the 5-day cap are forfeited.

Forfeited days are not paid out. The HR Portal shows your expected carryover from the autumn onwards, so you can plan to use the rest of your PTO before the end of the year. Carried-over days appear in your balance on January 1 and are used like any other PTO.

### 5.2 Leave over year-end

If you are on approved leave that spans December 31, you can carry over up to 10 unused PTO days instead of 5.

This applies to approved leave under the Leave of Absence Policy, such as parental leave, that is in progress on December 31. It is applied automatically when your leave is recorded in the HR Portal; you don't need to request it.

### 5.3 Leaving Ridgeline

If you leave Ridgeline, any payout of unused PTO is handled by HR at separation.

Plan your remaining PTO with your manager once you have given notice; approved PTO in your notice period is taken as normal. HR confirms your final balance and how it is handled in your final pay. Questions about your own situation go to the HR Service Desk.

## 6 Questions

Questions about this policy go to the HR Service Desk at hr@ridgeline.example or through HR Portal > Ask HR.

Before you ask, check your balance and the status of your requests in HR Portal > Time Off; most questions about how many days you have, or whether a request has been approved, are answered there. Questions about a specific request, such as whether a particular set of dates meets the notice rule or who needs to approve it, are best raised with your manager first. Questions about how this policy applies in an unusual situation, such as a change of Employment Type during the year or PTO around a leave of absence, go to the HR Service Desk, which replies within 2 working days.
