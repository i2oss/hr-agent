## 1 Purpose and Scope

This policy sets out where Ridgeline employees work: the three Work Arrangements, who can become remote-approved, how to request a change, the rules for working remotely, and temporary remote work away from your usual location. It applies to full-time, part-time and intern employees. Business travel is covered separately by RSE-LOC-01 §2.

Ridgeline believes engineering is best done with time together and time to focus. The arrangements in this policy aim to give teams regular in-person collaboration while allowing flexibility where the work allows it. Managers apply the policy fairly and consistently across their teams, and any questions go to the HR Service Desk.

## 2 Work Arrangements

### 2.1 Office-hybrid

Office-hybrid is the default Work Arrangement. Office-hybrid employees work from their home office on 3 anchor days a week, Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, and may work remotely on Monday and Friday. Teams plan meetings and collaboration for the anchor days.

Anchor days keep teams together for design reviews, coordination with other disciplines and mentoring of junior engineers. If you can't be in the office on an anchor day, for example because of a site visit or an appointment, tell your manager. Working from home on an occasional anchor day is fine; regularly missing them is not.

### 2.2 Remote-approved

Remote-approved employees work remotely most of the time. The arrangement must be approved by both the manager and HR. Remote-approved employees spend at least one week per quarter at their home office.

### 2.3 Field/site-based

Field/site-based employees work at project sites. Their roles cannot be done remotely, so they are not eligible for remote-approved status or for temporary remote work.

Field/site-based employees may still do some office-based tasks, such as writing reports, at a Ridgeline office when their manager agrees. If a field/site-based employee moves into an office-based role, their Work Arrangement changes with the role.

## 3 Who Can Become Remote-Approved

To become remote-approved you need at least 6 months of service at Ridgeline and no active performance plan. Field/site-based roles and interns are not eligible. For contractors, remote work is set by their contract.

Meeting these requirements makes you eligible to ask; it doesn't guarantee approval. Your manager and HR also consider whether your role can be done well remotely, the needs of your projects and clients, and your team's ability to work together.

## 4 Requesting a Change

To change your Work Arrangement to remote-approved, submit a request in the HR Portal. Your manager endorses it and HR approves it; HR gives a decision within 10 working days. Remote-approved arrangements are reviewed annually and can be revoked if the role or circumstances change.

Explain in your request where you will work, how you will meet core hours and how you will stay connected with your team. If the request is declined, HR tells you why. You can ask again later, for example when your circumstances or role change.

## 5 Working Remotely

### 5.1 Core hours

When working remotely, be available during core hours, 10:00–15:00 in your local time zone, as in the office (RSE-HB-01 §3.2).

### 5.2 Workspace and security

Your remote workspace must meet the security requirements in RSE-SEC-01 §5: use the VPN, keep screens private and never print Client Restricted documents at home.

Work in a space where others can't overhear confidential calls or see your screen, and lock your device when you step away. Use only company-managed devices for project work.

### 5.3 Equipment

Ridgeline provides your equipment and, where eligible, a home-office stipend; see the Equipment & Home-Office Stipend policy (RSE-EQ-01).

### 5.4 Office visits for remote-approved staff

Remote-approved employees spend at least one week per quarter at their home office, planned with their manager.

Visits are timed to be useful: project kick-offs, design reviews and team planning are good times. A visit week is a normal working week in the office and is not counted as business travel for cross-border purposes when the home office is in your own country. Travel for these visits is business travel and follows RSE-LOC-01 §2 and RSE-EXP-01.

## 6 Temporary Remote Work

### 6.1 What counts

Temporary remote work means working for a period from somewhere other than your usual work location, such as a family home or a holiday rental, while doing your normal job. Travelling to do Ridgeline work at a client, a project site or another Ridgeline office is business travel, not temporary remote work; see RSE-LOC-01 §2.

### 6.2 Within your state or province

Temporary remote work within the state or province of your usual work location is allowed. Tell your manager where you'll be working.

No approval is needed, but you must still meet core hours and, if you are office-hybrid, plan around your anchor days. For example, an Austin employee spending a week working from family in another Texas city just tells their manager.

### 6.3 Another US state or province

Temporary remote work from another US state, or another province, needs your manager's approval for up to 30 calendar days per calendar year in total. Anything over 30 calendar days in a year needs HR review: your manager can't approve it alone, and HR decides.

The 30 calendar days count every day you are away, including weekends, and they add up across all trips in the calendar year. For example, a stay from the first of one month to the tenth of the following month is well over 30 calendar days, so it needs HR review even if your manager supports it. Submit the request in the HR Portal with your dates and location; HR reviews longer stays because working in another state or province can affect payroll and tax registration.

### 6.4 Another country

Working from another country always needs HR review, whatever the length. See RSE-LOC-01 §3 for the rules and the Cross-Border Review.

Your manager can support the request, but cannot approve it. Plan well ahead: the review takes time, and there are notice and security requirements before you go.

### 6.5 Field/site roles

Field/site-based employees are not eligible for temporary remote work.

Their work depends on being at the project site. Travel to another site for work is business travel (RSE-LOC-01 §2).
