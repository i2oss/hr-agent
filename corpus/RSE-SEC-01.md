## 1 Scope and Ownership

### 1.1 Who this applies to

This policy applies to everyone with access to Ridgeline systems or information: employees of every Employment Type, and contractors. It covers Ridgeline devices, accounts and data wherever you work.

Engineering firms are attractive targets: client drawings, site surveys and bid information are valuable, and a single compromised account can expose many projects. Most incidents start with an everyday mistake, such as a reused password, a lost laptop or a document sent to the wrong address. The rules in this policy are designed to make those mistakes rare and to limit the harm when they happen.

### 1.2 Policy owner

The policy is owned by Ines Moreau, Head of Information Security. Questions go to IT Security.

IT Security reviews this policy every year and when new threats or tools appear. Managers are responsible for making sure their teams know and follow it, and project leads for making sure client requirements on data handling are understood by everyone on the project.

## 2 Data Classification

Ridgeline classifies information in four levels. Public information is approved for anyone to see, such as published project case studies. Internal information is for Ridgeline staff, such as office announcements and internal procedures. Confidential information could harm Ridgeline or its people if disclosed, such as bids, fee proposals and HR records. Client Restricted information belongs to a client and is covered by a client agreement: engineering drawings, calculations, client models and site surveys are Client Restricted. Handle each level according to this policy, and when in doubt, treat information as Client Restricted.

Label documents with their classification where your tools allow it, and share Confidential and Client Restricted information only through approved Ridgeline systems, never through personal email or personal file-sharing accounts. Some clients set stricter rules in their agreements, such as storing their data only in a particular country; the project lead tells the team when that applies.

## 3 Devices

### 3.1 Company-managed devices

Confidential and Client Restricted data may only be stored or worked on using company-managed devices: the laptops and tablets Ridgeline issues, which IT keeps encrypted and up to date.

Don't disable security software, change device settings that IT manages, or let anyone else use your Ridgeline device. Install updates when prompted; IT may restart devices that fall too far behind.

### 3.2 Personal devices

Personal devices may be used only for browser access to webmail and the HR Portal, and only with MFA. Personal phones may be used for Ridgeline email and chat only if they are enrolled in Ridgeline mobile management.

### 3.3 Personal laptops

Never use a personal laptop to store, open or work on Ridgeline project files, whether at home, in the office or travelling.

This applies even to a quick look at a drawing or a short edit to a calculation. Personal laptops are outside IT's management, so Ridgeline cannot protect, track or wipe the files on them. If you don't have a Ridgeline laptop with you when you need one, ask IT about a loan device rather than using your own.

### 3.4 Lost or stolen devices

Report a lost or stolen Ridgeline device, or a personal phone enrolled in mobile management, to IT Security within 1 hour of noticing, so the device can be locked or wiped.

Report it even if you think the device may turn up again. If it was stolen, report the theft to the local police as well and give IT the report reference.

## 4 Accounts and Access

MFA is required on every Ridgeline account. Lock your screen whenever you step away; devices lock automatically after 5 minutes. Never share your account or password, and never use someone else's account.

Use a different password for each system, and never reuse a Ridgeline password on a personal site. Shared accounts are not allowed, even for project tools; ask IT to set up proper access for each person. When someone leaves a project, the project lead asks IT to remove their access to it.

## 5 Working Remotely and Travelling

### 5.1 VPN

Always connect through the Ridgeline VPN when working off-site, including at home, on project sites and when travelling.

The VPN protects project data on networks Ridgeline doesn't control. If the VPN won't connect, stop working with Confidential and Client Restricted data and contact IT. Don't use another person's hotspot or network to get around a VPN problem.

### 5.2 Public Wi-Fi

Use public Wi-Fi, such as in airports, hotels and cafes, only with the VPN connected.

Prefer your phone's hotspot to an open network where you can. Be aware of who can see your screen in public places, and avoid confidential calls where you can be overheard.

### 5.3 Printing at home

Never print Client Restricted documents at home.

Print other documents at home only when necessary, and shred them when you're done. Use Ridgeline offices for large-format printing of drawings.

### 5.4 Working from another country

Notify IT Security at least 5 working days before you start working from another country. IT Security will check that your device and access are set up for the destination, and may issue a travel laptop for the trip. This applies in addition to the HR review in RSE-LOC-01 §3.

Some destinations have rules on encryption or on carrying certain data, and some client agreements forbid access from particular countries. IT Security checks these before you go. Don't take Client Restricted paper documents abroad, and keep your devices with you while travelling. Business trips abroad follow the same notification rule when you will work on Ridgeline systems while away.

## 6 Acceptable Use

### 6.1 Personal use

Limited, reasonable personal use of Ridgeline devices and accounts is allowed, provided it doesn't interfere with your work or break any other policy.

### 6.2 AI tools

Never put Confidential or Client Restricted data, including client drawings, models or calculations, into public AI tools such as public chatbots. Use only the AI tools that IT has approved for that data.

Public AI tools may store what you type and use it to train future models, so anything you paste in could be seen by others. Public information can be used with public AI tools. Always check AI output yourself before relying on it in engineering work; you remain responsible for every calculation and drawing you sign off.

### 6.3 Installing software

Install software only from the approved software catalogue. Ask IT to review anything else before installing it.

This includes browser extensions, plug-ins for design software and free tools downloaded from the internet. IT reviews requests promptly and adds approved tools to the catalogue for everyone.

## 7 Incident Reporting

### 7.1 Report within 1 hour to IT Security

Report any suspected security incident, such as a phishing email you clicked, a lost device or data sent to the wrong person, within 1 hour to IT Security at security@ridgeline.example or on +1 555 0142, available 24/7.

Report even if you aren't sure it is an incident: a false alarm costs little, while a delay can let a problem spread. Don't try to investigate or fix it yourself, and don't delete suspicious emails until IT Security has seen them. You won't be blamed for reporting an honest mistake promptly.

## 8 Consequences

Breaking this policy can lead to removal of access to Ridgeline systems and to action under the HR process in the Employee Handbook (RSE-HB-01).

For contractors, a breach may also end their engagement under their contract. IT Security looks at what happened and why before deciding what to do; an honest mistake reported promptly is treated very differently from deliberate or repeated misuse.
